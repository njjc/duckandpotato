July 2014 note: I wrote this whole post when we were in France last year, and never posted it. I was going to write a new post in honor of a wonderful, lengthy honeymoon to Europe with my very-nearly-not-pre-husband-anymore-but-in-fact-actual-regular-husband, but this post has a lot going for it already. - Cat We did a lot of things in Paris. Climbed the Eiffel Tower. I don't have any pictures of this, because I was trying not to cry. Also, there were signs and MANY LOUD announcements to alert people to the fact that PICKPOCKETS ARE ACTIVE ON THE TOWER. So, there are two "floors" in the Eiffel Tower, and then the actual tower, at the very top. You can climb stairs to the first two floors (please see earlier reference to crying), but then you (and 128492384 other tourists from funny places such as the Netherlands) take an elevator to the very top. There is NO WAY up there, other than the elevator. Apparently, there are active pickpockets on the tower. Having given some thought to a life of crime, it seems to me that if you were to pick someone's pocket, you'd like an easily accessible escape route. Not, say, to have to WAIT IN A 20 MINUTE LINE TO TAKE AN ELEVATOR BACK DOWN TO EARTH FIRST. Walked through Notre Dame cathedral. I totally get how Catholicism won over medieval peasants. If you're considering starting a religion and want to impress upon the peasants the power of your deity, I suggest gilding things. Had a picnic on the Seine. Nic can solve literally any problem that involves booze. We bought a bottle of wine but didn't have a corkscrew, so he pushed the cork into the bottle. Also he can open beer bottles with anything, I think including his mind. Walked down the Champs-Élysées, and around the Arc de Triomphe. I don't remember this because I was distracted at the time by blisters on my heels the size of bagels. To the lady who sold me those "comfortable" flats, I would like to say "SOFT LEATHER, MY FAT, WHITE ASS". Went to the Louvre, fulfilling a lifelong dream of touching all the art in a museum. Tried at length never to say aloud the word "Louvre" because it's really hard to pronounce. Also looked for false doors and secret passages in Louis XIII's grand salon. You KNOW they have those. Mostly, spent time in the Egyptian antiquities rooms. You're welcome, self ages 7-15.
