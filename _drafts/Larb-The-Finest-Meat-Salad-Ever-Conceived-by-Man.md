I don't know if I'd say larb is my favorite dish, because khao soi exists, but I love it a lot. Nic says he is willing to eat it every day forever, which is a pretty good endorsement. Real larb is not easy to make. I made it (almost) authentically once and I ended up chopping duck for over an hour. Even this recipe you're about to read for lazy-ass larb is kind of a pain, as it requires that you make two pain-in-the-ass things to put in it. And honestly, those two ingredients are not optional. Larb just doesn't taste the same without toasted chile powder and toasted rice powder. The rice powder in particular is a bitch, but just make a bunch at once and then you'll have it for months. Thai food is like anything in that you need some specific ingredients on hand to make it, but once you have them, much of the vast ocean of deliciousness that is Thai food becomes available to you. Furthermore, I think homemade Thai food becomes truly transcendent if you're willing to do one big trip to an Asian market and then make big batches of useful things and freeze them into meal-sized portions for use whenever you need. Here are some things that I keep on hand, frozen in small quantities in ziploc bags: green curry paste, khao soi curry paste, pre-chopped lemongrass, pre-chopped makrut lime leaves, satay marinade, shrimp paste, etc. Making each thing individually is not at all hard. You either throw everything immediately into a food processor, or roast everything and then throw it into a food processor. THAT IS IT. Before you go back to looking at Instagram because I'm discouraging your interest in this by saying it's hard, this larb recipe is really not that hard. I would say it takes about an hour of effort, but you can ride that hour for at least 2-3 meals. I make a big batch of the meat part, portion it into multiple meals, and assemble the remaining larb components fresh each time I want to eat it. Larb is 4 things: the meat, the dressing, the herbs/toppings, and what you serve with it. Within that framework, larb is whatever you want it to be, and most especially, whatever you might have on hand. I make larb differently every time I make it, because I have a terrible memory, but this is the version I like most. Larb **Meat component** 1-2 tbsp of avocado or other high-heat friendly vegetable oil 2 lb ground meat (I use all pork, or pork and turkey, or sometimes duck. You can use whatever you want. I've eaten fish larb in restaurants but haven't made it, a problem I will rectify very soon.) 3-4 cloves garlic, minced 3 tbsp lemongrass, sliced very thin 3-4 tbsp fish sauce 2 tbsp brown sugar 2 tbsp soy sauce or Thai seasoning sauce if you have it 1 tbsp toasted chile powder Method: Heat the oil in a big cast iron pan on medium-high. Pop the meat in there and smoosh/mash it into an even layer and let it brown on the bottom for 2-3 minutes. Move the meat all around again, then let it brown again for 2-3 minutes. Now mix in all the rest of the ingredients and move it around again. Keep letting it cook for a minute or two, then moving everything and smooshing it, for maybe 10-12 more minutes, or until the liquid is all evaporated and the meat chunks look a little caramelized. Put the meat in another dish (one that is not hot) and let it cool to room temperature. **Dressing component** 3 cloves garlic, minced 1 tbsp toasted chile powder 2-3 tbsp white sugar or palm sugar if you have it zest of 1 lime juice of 2 limes Method: Whisk this all together in a bowl and set it aside. **Herbs and toppings component** 2-3 shallots, peeled, halved and sliced very thinly half a bunch of cilantro, leaves and stems, chopped a few sprigs of mint, leaves chopped 3 green onions, sliced thinly 2 tbsp toasted sticky rice powder Optional: 2 tbsp fried shallots (these come from a convenient jar), 2 tbsp fried garlic (ditto) Method: Put all these into separate piles because they go onto the larb in a specific order. **Final method for combining all the larb components:** Spread the meat in an even layer in a nice pretty dish. Spread the thinly sliced shallots on top of the meat and lightly toss the two together with a spoon. Then, pour the dressing evenly over everything, then lightly toss with a spoon until incorporated. Then spread the cilantro, mint and green onion in an even layer, then lightly toss with a spoon until incorporated. Then sprinkle the toasted rice powder evenly over everything, then the shallots and fried garlic and DO NOT mix those in, they should lie prettily on top when you serve it. **To serve alongside your larb** Sticky rice (do not substitute regular white rice, because it's much less delicious than sticky rice so just buy sticky rice. It's not hard to make - you can use a steamer or, incredibly, the microwave - and larb really requires it) Large green cabbage, cored and sliced into wedges to use kinda like lettuce wraps Some other salad-ish thing if you want, most recently I made a cucumber salad (2 cucumbers, seeded and chopped, 1 shallot halved and sliced thinly. Dressing = 3 tbsp sweet Thai chili sauce + 2 tbsp chile garlic sauce + 1 tbsp soy sauce + 3 tbsp white vinegar + maybe a bit less than 1/4 cup hot water). **Toasted Chile Powder** Get a bunch of Thai green chiles (birdseye) and dry them until they're red. Just leave them on a dish out in a sunny spot for awhile. Once they're dried, stem them. Then get a cast iron pan hot on medium-low heat and dry fry the chiles (while moving them around pretty frequently) until they're browned and almost charred-looking. Let them cool, then blitz them in a spice grinder until fine. Keep in a jar forever. Caution- it's HOT. **Toasted Sticky Rice Powder** Take a cup of dry, unwashed sticky rice and do exactly the same process as above: dry fry it in a cast iron pan on medium-low heat until it's golden brown. Always be stirring it. It'll take maybe 30 minutes to get it golden-y enough. Then let it cool and blitz it into a powder in a spice grinder, then store it in a jar forever.
